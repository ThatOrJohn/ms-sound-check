About the data

Sound Check turns public monitoring data into a plain-language read on the Mississippi Sound: how salty and how warm the water is, how hard the rivers are running, how much rain has fallen upstream, and whether any of it is unusual for the time of year. It updates every three hours.

Where the numbers come from

WhatSource
Salinity and water temperatureUSGS stations operated with the Mississippi Department of Marine Resources: Merrill Shell Bank Light, Round Island Light, Point Cadet in Biloxi's Back Bay, and the East Pearl River.
River flowUSGS gauges on the Pearl River near Bogalusa and the Pascagoula River at Merrill.
Air temperatureNational Weather Service observations at Gulfport-Biloxi International Airport.
Rain, observed and forecastOpen-Meteo. Basin totals average a few points across each watershed, so treat them as approximate.

Salinity is calculated from the stations' conductivity readings with the standard seawater formula (PSS-78). That gives a longer and more complete history than the separate salinity series, which starts in 2011. Two stations that used to report salinity, Gulfport Light and East Ship Island Light, stopped in 2024.

What "typical" means

For each station and each day of the year, we look at every reading within a week of that date in every year on record (back to 1998 at the oldest stations). "Typical" is the middle of those readings. "Unusual" means outside the middle 80%. On the charts, the shaded band is that middle 80%.

What the alerts mean

  • Note: a reading is unusual for the time of year. Worth knowing, not a problem by itself.
  • Watch, Warning, Alert: a condition that matters is building. The main one today is freshwater stress on oysters: research by the Northern Gulf Institute and the University of Southern Mississippi after the 2019 Bonnet Carré Spillway opening found oysters die when salinity stays below about 2 ppt for more than two weeks. We raise a watch at 5 days, a warning at 10, and an alert at 14.
  • Shrimp season lean: in spring, warmer water has tended to mean an earlier brown shrimp opening. Across 2012–2026 that relationship cut the typical miss from about 8 days to about 6, and some years missed by weeks. It's a lean, not a forecast. MDMR sets the date from its own trawl sampling.

Caveats

Readings are provisional until USGS reviews them, and instruments foul or drift. Readings that are physically implausible for the Sound, such as water saltier than the open Gulf, are shown with a caveat and never trigger alerts. If a station stops reporting, its card says so rather than showing old numbers as current.

This is a hobby project, not an official source. For closures, seasons and regulations, go to MDMR. Station details are on each station page.
